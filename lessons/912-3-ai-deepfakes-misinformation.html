🤖 AI, Deepfakes & Misinformation in the Digital Age

Grade: 9-12
Duration: 45 min
🎯 Objectives
  • Understand how AI and deepfakes work
  • Develop critical thinking to evaluate information credibility
  • Recognize misinformation tactics
  • Understand the real-world consequences of AI-generated disinformation
📖 Lesson Content

What Is a Deepfake?

Definition: AI-generated fake video or audio that makes someone appear to say/do something they didn't.

How it works: AI learns from thousands of photos/videos to recreate someone's face, voice, and mannerisms convincingly.

The danger:

  • Can be used to frame someone (false evidence)
  • Can defame someone (put them in compromising situations)
  • Can manipulate elections (fake politician statements)
  • Can damage relationships and reputations

Misinformation vs. Disinformation

Misinformation: False information spread without intent to deceive (honest mistake)

Disinformation: False information spread INTENTIONALLY to deceive

Examples:

  • Fake news articles designed to manipulate emotions
  • Cherry-picked quotes taken out of context
  • AI-generated images/videos presented as real
  • Conspiracy theories spread as fact

How to Evaluate Information

The SIFT method (4 quick checks):

  1. Stop: Before sharing, pause and think "Is this real?"
  2. Investigate the source: Who published this? Are they credible? What's their bias?
  3. Find better coverage: Do other credible sources report the same thing?
  4. Trace the claim: Where did this originate? Is it being taken out of context?

Red Flags for Misinformation

  • 🚩 Extreme emotional language ("SHOCKING!" "YOU WON'T BELIEVE!")
  • 🚩 Unverifiable claims or vague sources
  • 🚩 Only one source reporting it (real news gets multiple sources)
  • 🚩 Images/videos without context
  • 🚩 Requests to "share before it gets deleted"
  • 🚩 Attacks on the messenger rather than addressing the claim
  • 🚩 Unusual grammar/spelling (many disinformation campaigns come from non-native speakers)

Activity: Spot the Fake

Show examples (real vs. AI-generated images, credible vs. fake articles).

Have students identify: Is this real? How can you tell? What red flags do you see?

Tools to help detect fakes:

  • Google Reverse Image Search (find the original source)
  • Snopes.com (fact-checking site)
  • FactCheck.org
  • Credible news sources (Reuters, AP, BBC, NPR)
💡 Key Takeaway

The future of information will be harder to navigate. AI gets better every month. But critical thinking—asking questions, checking sources, thinking before sharing—will always matter.

Be a skeptic. That's not cynicism. That's wisdom.

Family Discussion: Truth in the Digital Age

Questions for Your Teen

  • "What's something you've seen online recently that you weren't sure was real?"
  • "How do you decide what to trust? What sources do you find credible?"
  • "How has social media changed the way information spreads? Is that good or bad?"

What Parents Can Do

Model critical thinking. When you see a claim, ask questions out loud: "Where did this come from? Who benefits from this being true?" Show your teen that healthy skepticism isn't cynicism—it's intelligence.

The Real Danger

Not just deepfakes, but the erosion of trust itself. When people can't agree on basic facts, it's hard to solve problems together. Help your teen be part of the solution by thinking carefully and sharing responsibly.
