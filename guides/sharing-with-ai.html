What Should I Share With AI?

AI becomes more useful when you give it context. That makes understanding what you're sharing more important, not less.

Last reviewed: September 2026

What is this?

When you use AI tools—ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, or others—you're sharing information with them.

That information might be:

  • Ideas you're thinking through
  • Passwords or account numbers (accidentally or intentionally)
  • Medical information
  • Financial details
  • Information about other people
  • Proprietary or confidential work information
  • Personal stories or feelings

AI tools work better when you give them context. But that context can include sensitive information.

Understanding what you're sharing—and why—puts you in control of your choices.

The Framework: NEED → MINIMIZE → CHECK SETTINGS

NEED

Does the AI tool actually need this information to help you?

MINIMIZE

Share what the tool needs, not everything you have. Redact names, account numbers, identifying details when you can.

CHECK SETTINGS

Review the tool's privacy settings. Understand what happens to what you share.

This is how you share intentionally. You're giving the AI what it needs to help you, while keeping unnecessary information private.

Information Sensitivity Levels

Lower concern doesn't mean risk-free. What makes sense to share still depends on the tool, your settings and what you're trying to do.

Lower Concern

Public information, general questions, brainstorming, basic information already shared publicly. Examples: "How do I make pasta?" or general information about a public figure.

Think First

Personal preferences, opinions, descriptions of situations where privacy matters. Examples: Health concerns, work situations, family dynamics. These are reasonable to share depending on context and tool, but consider: Do I want this information used to train the model? Will I regret sharing this later?

Higher Sensitivity

Passwords, Social Security numbers, account information, financial details, medical information, information about other people without their consent, proprietary or confidential work information. These generally should not be shared with AI tools unless absolutely necessary for a specific task, and you've considered the risks.

Use this framework with each tool you use. Different tools have different protections and different default settings.

What should I know?

Training is one privacy question. It is not the only one.

When people ask "Will AI train on what I share?", they're asking a real question. But there are others:

  • Is the conversation retained or deleted?
  • Can humans review what I shared?
  • Are connected apps accessing other information?
  • Can I access or delete my data later?
  • Where is my data stored?

Different tools handle these differently. Check the tool's privacy settings and documentation.

Connected apps can share more than you realize

Many AI tools can connect to other apps and services—email, calendar, cloud storage, browsing history, photos.

Ask yourself:

  • What can it access?
  • What will it do with that access?
  • Can I disconnect it later?

You can usually turn off connected-app permissions in the tool's settings.

Public sharing is different from private

If you use a "share link" or "public share" button in an AI tool, your conversation (or part of it) becomes public.

Remember: Private conversation + public share button = potentially public information.

What can I do?

ChatGPT Settings (OpenAI)

ChatGPT has a "Data Controls" setting in Settings.

  • "Improve the model for everyone": When this is ON, your chats may be used to train OpenAI models. You can turn it OFF. This is a personal-user setting.
  • Memory: ChatGPT can remember details about you across conversations. This is separate from model training. You can disable Memory if you prefer.
  • Temporary Chat: You can create a Temporary Chat that is retained for up to 30 days, then automatically deleted. These are not used for model training while temporary. Temporary Chats can still use your existing memory or personalization if you're personalized.

Check your Data Controls to review what settings match your preferences.

Google Gemini Settings

Google's Gemini has a "Keep Activity" setting.

  • Keep Activity ON: Your chats are saved and may be used to improve Google's services and AI models. This is the default.
  • Keep Activity OFF: Future chats are not used to train Google's AI models (except when you submit feedback). Turning off Keep Activity also means future chats won't appear in your Gemini Apps Activity, and some features that rely on saved activity or Connected Apps may become unavailable.

Check your Gemini Privacy settings to change this preference.

General Strategies

  • Don't paste full passwords or account numbers. Share just what's needed or redact sensitive parts.
  • Use pseudonyms instead of real names when describing situations.
  • Review connected-app permissions regularly. Disconnect any you're not using.
  • If you're sharing something with higher sensitivity, consider whether a public AI tool is the right choice, or whether the tool's privacy protections are sufficient for your situation.

Put it in context

Share intentionally. Give AI enough context to help you without automatically giving it everything available.

That's not about being paranoid. It's about being intentional.

You wouldn't tell a stranger at the bus stop your password. You wouldn't email your medical history to a random business. And you wouldn't automatically share every detail of your life in a conversation with someone new.

The same principle applies to AI tools:

Share what the tool needs to help you. Keep everything else private until you understand why you're sharing it.

Do I have the right to share this information with this tool? Is this information mine to share? Different answers lead to different choices.

That's how you stay in control.
