📱 Social Media & Your Digital Reputation

Grade: 6-8
Duration: Multi-lesson unit (2-3 sessions)
Topic: Digital Footprint & Online Identity
🎯 Objectives
  • Understand that social media creates a permanent digital record
  • Recognize how algorithms shape what they see and how feeds are designed to be addictive
  • Evaluate impact of posts on digital reputation (college, jobs, relationships)
  • Make intentional choices about what to share
⏱️ Timeline (Multi-session: 45 min per session)
Session 1: The Algorithm & Why You See What You See (15 min teach + 30 min activity)
Session 2: Digital Permanence & Future Consequences (15 min teach + 30 min debate)
Session 3: Managing Your Reputation (15 min teach + 30 min personal action plan)
📖 Lesson Content

Session 1: The Algorithm

Key Idea: Social media apps are designed to keep you scrolling. Algorithms learn what you like and show you more of it—even if it's not good for you.

  • How it works: Every like, comment, share trains the algorithm about your interests
  • The goal: Keep you engaged (= more ads = more profit)
  • The risk: You can get stuck in a "bubble" seeing only one perspective
  • Comparison trap: You see highlight reels, not reality, leading to low self-esteem

Activity: Have students track what they're shown for 1 week. Patterns emerge. Algorithm is watching!

Session 2: Digital Permanence

Reality Check: Posts can be deleted, but someone always screenshots.

  • Colleges check social media during admissions
  • Employers Google applicants before hiring
  • Embarrassing posts from age 13 can resurface at age 18
  • Things meant to be private get shared

The question to ask before posting: "Would I want my parents, teacher, or future employer seeing this?"

Activity: Debate scenario: "My friend wants me to post a silly video. I'm worried it'll be used against me later. What should I do?"

Session 3: Managing Reputation

Steps to a healthy digital reputation:

  1. Audit what's already online about you (Google yourself)
  2. Delete or hide old posts you regret
  3. Adjust privacy settings (friends-only, not public)
  4. Think before posting (Is this kind? Permanent? Safe?)
  5. Balance: Social media + real life activities

Activity: Personal action plan. "What will I do to manage my digital reputation?"

Family Discussion: Social Media & Reputation

Questions for Dinner Table

  • "What's the difference between what people show online and what's really true?"
  • "If you posted something in middle school, would you want colleges to see it?"
  • "How do you feel after scrolling social media? Energized or drained?"

Parent Action

Model healthy social media use. Limit your own screen time. Don't overshare about your kids. Show that you think before posting.
