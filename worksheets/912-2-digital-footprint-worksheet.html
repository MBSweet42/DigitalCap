Activity 1: Future Impact Scenario Analysis
Each post you make now has a future. Analyze how these scenarios could impact real life:
Case Study 1: The Twitter Archive
A 14-year-old posted tweets with racial slurs they didn't mean seriously. At age 20, applying to college, old tweets resurface on Twitter. The college sees them during admissions review and rejects their application.

Could this happen to you?
What's the lesson?
Case Study 2: The Employer Search
A high schooler posts explicit photos on their Instagram. Years later at 22, applying for an internship, the employer Googles their name and finds old Instagram posts. They don't get the job.

Consequences:
How could this have been prevented?
Case Study 3: The Scholarship Impact
A mean post you made about a classmate at 14 resurfaces when you're applying for scholarships at 18. The post gets 100,000 shares. You lose the scholarship. You apologize, but the damage is done.

What's the real cost here?
Activity 2: Your Digital Footprint Audit
Reflect on your current online presence (without sharing private account details):
1. What platforms do you use the most?
2. If a college admissions officer or future employer searched your name, what would they find?
3. Are there any old posts, photos, or comments you'd want to delete or make private?
4. Privacy settings check: Which accounts are public vs. private?
5. Before you post something, what question will you ask yourself moving forward?
Activity 3: The Long Game - Timeline Reflection
Think about your digital footprint across different life stages:
1 year from now: What you post now will be remembered by your friends.
What do you want them to remember about you online?
2-5 years from now: You're applying to college. Admissions see your social media.
What would you want them to see?
5-10 years from now: You're applying for your first real job/career. Employers Google you.
What impression do you want to give?
