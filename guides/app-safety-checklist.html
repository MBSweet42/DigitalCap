How to Evaluate an App
Find information about any app, understand what it can do, and decide what makes sense for you.

CONFIRM YOU HAVE THE RIGHT APP

Before investigating, make sure you're researching the correct app.

Check these details:
• Exact app name (spelling matters)
• Developer or company name
• App icon/logo (can you see it?)
• Official app-store listing (Apple App Store or Google Play)
• Official website if the developer has one

Why? Apps can have similar names. Copycat or outdated listings exist. Researching the wrong version will produce misleading information.

UNDERSTAND WHAT THE APP DOES

Before evaluating exposure, understand the app's purpose and how it works.

Ask yourself:
• What is this app designed to do?
• Is it social (connecting people)?
• Does it include messaging or chat?
• Does it include user-generated content?
• Does it connect to the open web?
• Does it use location?
• Does it involve AI or recommendations?
• Does it include purchases or upgrades?
• Can people upload or share content?

A capability is not automatically a problem. The goal is to understand what is possible, then decide if that fits your needs.

CHECK PERMISSIONS

Apps request permission to access your device's features. Understanding why an app needs each permission helps you make an informed choice.

Common permissions to consider:
• Camera (for video calls, photos)
• Microphone (for audio/calls)
• Location (GPS or approximate location)
• Contacts (access to your contact list)
• Photos & Media (access to your files)
• Notifications (alerts to your screen)
• Calendar or Health data (if applicable)

Key question: Does this permission make sense for the feature I want to use?

Example: A video-call app reasonably needs camera and microphone access for calls to work. A map app may need location for navigation. A simple note-taking app requesting precise location would deserve investigation.

Most device settings let you review or change permissions later. You don't necessarily have to say yes to every permission to use an app.

LOOK AT PRIVACY & DATA

Find and review what information the app collects and how it's used.

Look for:
• Privacy policy (usually linked on app-store page or developer website)
• What information is collected (account data, usage, location, etc.)
• Why it's collected (essential vs. optional)
• Whether data is used for advertising or personalization
• Whether information is shared with third parties
• Whether an account is required
• How to delete your information or remove your account

Privacy policies can be difficult to read. Useful questions: Can I find it? Does it identify what's collected? Does it explain why? Does it explain who it's shared with?

Start with official sources (app-store listing, developer website, official help pages) rather than third-party summaries.

COMMUNICATION & OTHER USERS

If an app connects people, understand how contact and communication work.

Consider:
• Can users communicate with other users?
• Who can start a conversation (friends only, anyone, etc.)?
• Are messages public or private?
• Can messages disappear or be deleted?
• Can someone block another user?
• Can someone report inappropriate behavior or content?
• Is there moderation by the company?
• Can someone control who contacts them?

Communicating with people you don't already know is not automatically harmful. Exposure depends on who can start contact, available privacy controls, whether reporting/blocking tools exist, moderation practices, and how the app is being used.

CONTENT & EXTERNAL ACCESS

Understand how content reaches you and where it can lead.

Investigate:
• Is content chosen by you, recommended by an algorithm, or both?
• Is user-generated content available?
• Can people search broadly for content?
• Are content controls or filters available?
• Can links lead outside the app?
• Is there an in-app browser?
• Can embedded video or web content appear inside the app?

An app may appear to have a limited purpose while still providing access to broader content through links, browsers, videos, or chat. Understanding these pathways helps you make an informed choice.

PURCHASES, SUBSCRIPTIONS & ADVERTISING

Understand potential costs and advertising before downloading.

Check:
• Does the app cost money to download?
• Are there in-app purchases? What do they cost?
• Are there subscriptions? Do they auto-renew?
• Is there free trial or paid trial access?
• Is there premium currency or special items for purchase?
• Does the app display advertising?
• Are there spending controls or parental controls?

The official app-store listing usually shows pricing and purchase options. Review before downloading to understand potential costs.

SAFEGUARDS & CONTROLS

Investigate what tools exist to manage your experience and protect yourself.

Look for:
• Privacy settings (who can see your profile, posts, etc.)
• Communication controls (who can contact you)
• Block and report tools
• Content controls or filters
• Notification settings (what alerts you get)
• Location controls
• Account security features (password reset, 2-factor authentication, etc.)
• Parental or guardian controls if available

Safeguards can change how exposure works, but no setting guarantees perfect protection. Investigate what tools exist to help you maintain control.

CHECK MORE THAN ONE SOURCE

Compare information from multiple credible sources before deciding.

Start with official sources:
• App-store listing
• Developer website
• Official privacy policy
• Official help/support pages
Then check additional sources:
• Established digital-safety organizations
• Professional reviews and journalism
• Community experiences and discussions
• User reviews on the app store

User reviews can reveal patterns and real experiences. A single viral post, TikTok, or Reddit comment should not automatically be treated as proof. Look for consensus across multiple sources instead of relying on one story.

PUT IT TOGETHER: UNDERSTAND YOUR CHOICE

Use this framework to organize what you've learned:

CAPABILITY: What can this app do?
What features, access, and connections does it enable?
EXPOSURE: What could that expose me to?
What information is collected? Who might contact me? What content could I encounter? Can it send me outside the app?
SAFEGUARDS: What controls exist?
What privacy settings, blocking tools, content filters, or parental controls are available?
CONTEXT: What matters in my situation?
My age, needs, maturity, how I plan to use it, who I know on it, my experience with similar apps.
CHOICE: Does this make sense for me?
Given everything above, what choice feels right?

Important: Two people can reasonably make different choices about the same app. What works for one person may not work for another.

💡 KEY PRINCIPLE
Evaluating an app is about understanding what it can do and what that means for you—not about assigning a universal "safe" or "unsafe" label. The same app can be a good fit for one person and not for another, depending on their age, needs, maturity, and situation.
