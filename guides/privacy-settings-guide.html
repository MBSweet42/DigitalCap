Review Your Privacy & Contact Settings
Understand what common privacy controls do and choose the settings that fit how you use your accounts.
START WITH WHAT YOU WANT TO CONTROL

Privacy settings vary across platforms, but they usually address a few key areas. Think about which of these matter most to you or the account holder:

Who can see posts, profile, and content
Most platforms let you control whether your account is public or private, and who can see individual posts. Consider who you want to share content with and whether you're comfortable with that audience.
Who can contact or message you
Messaging controls let you limit direct contact to friends, followers, or specific groups. You can also handle message requests separately—messages from people you don't follow often go into a separate inbox.
Location sharing
Some services share location through posts, check-ins, maps, or dedicated features. You can control whether your location appears in content and who can see it. Device-level permissions also affect whether apps can access your location.
Activity and online status
Many apps show whether you're currently online or when you were last active. You can often disable this so only you know when you're using the app.
Tags, mentions, and comments
You can usually control whether people can tag you in posts or comments, and whether tagged content shows on your profile or requires approval first.
Connected apps and permissions
Apps you've connected to your account may have access to your data or the ability to post on your behalf. Periodically review and remove apps you no longer use.
Device permissions (camera, microphone, location, contacts)
Your phone or computer controls which apps can access camera, microphone, location, photos, and contacts. You can allow access for the features you use and deny it for the rest.
Purchase and account protections
You can require passwords or authentication before purchases, and control recovery options for account access.
Notifications
Notifications are designed to bring you back to an app. You can reduce or disable them if they feel distracting or create pressure to respond immediately.
Account discovery and contact syncing
Some apps let others find you by phone number, email, or by syncing device contacts. You can control whether your account shows up in search and whether to allow contact syncing.
PUBLIC VS. PRIVATE ACCOUNTS

The difference matters—and the right choice depends on your goals.

Private accounts
Limit who can follow the account and see posts. Good if you want to control your audience and keep content among people you choose.
Tradeoff: You may need to approve follow requests, and people can't discover you as easily.
Public accounts
Anyone can see posts and follow. This can make sense for creators, businesses, organizations, or people intentionally sharing publicly. It can also mean more visibility and reach.
Tradeoff: Your content is visible to a wider audience, including people you don't know.

The important part is understanding who can see the information and being comfortable with that choice.

MESSAGES AND CONTACT

Most platforms offer options like:

Everyone
Anyone can send you messages.
Followers or friends
Only people you follow back or who follow you can message you directly.
Message requests or filtered inboxes
Messages from people you don't know go into a separate folder you can review without seeing notifications.
Limited or nobody
Only people you explicitly allow can contact you, or messaging is disabled entirely.

Choose the option that fits how you use the platform and who you want to hear from.

LOCATION SHARING

Location information can be shared in several ways:

  • In posts or check-ins: You tag a location when you share content.
  • Metadata in photos: Photos taken with a phone often contain location data.
  • Dedicated location features: Some apps let you share your location with friends or on a map.
  • Device permissions: Apps can request access to your device's location (GPS).

Key questions to ask:

  • Does the app need location access for features you use?
  • Who can see your location—friends only, followers, or anyone?
  • Is location sharing continuous or temporary?
  • Can you turn it off easily?

Review your location settings to ensure they match who you want to share that information with.

DEVICE PERMISSIONS

Your phone or computer controls which apps can access certain hardware and data. Common permissions include:

Location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos
Apps may need these for features like video calls, photo sharing, or location-based services.
Bluetooth and local network
Some apps need access to connect to nearby devices or your home network.

Principle: Allow the access you need for the features you use. If an app doesn't need a permission for how you use it, consider limiting it. You can often change permissions later if you need a feature.

NOTIFICATIONS

Notifications are designed to bring you back to an app and keep you informed. If they feel distracting or create pressure to respond immediately, you can reduce or disable some of them:

  • Turn off non-essential notifications
  • Disable sound or vibration alerts
  • Set specific times when notifications are allowed
  • Disable badges that show unread counts

You're in control of how much each app interrupts you.

FOR PARENTS AND CAREGIVERS

If you're helping someone else review privacy settings, consider:

Age, maturity, and independence
Privacy and supervision expectations often change as a young person grows. What makes sense at 10 may be different at 13 or 16.
The purpose of the account
Is it for messaging friends, sharing hobbies, gaming, learning, or something else? The purpose can affect which settings matter most.
Household expectations and values
Different families have different comfort levels with privacy and visibility online. Your choices should reflect what makes sense for your household.
Consider discussing:
  • Who should be able to contact them and how
  • What information stays private
  • Location sharing and when it's useful
  • Purchases and spending limits
  • What to do when something feels uncomfortable or confusing
  • How to ask for help without fear of losing device access immediately
PLATFORM EXAMPLES

Instagram

Consider reviewing:

  • Account privacy (public vs. private)
  • Message requests and who can message you
  • Story audience and visibility
  • Tags and mentions
  • Activity status visibility
  • Location permissions on your device

Consider reviewing:

  • Account privacy settings
  • Who can message you

Snapchat

Consider reviewing:

  • Who can view your story

Discord

Facebook and Messenger

Consider reviewing:

DEVICE-LEVEL PRIVACY

Your phone or computer has settings that affect all apps:

iPhone (iOS)

Look for:

  • Settings → Privacy → Location Services, Camera, Microphone, Contacts
  • Settings → App Store → Require password for purchases
  • Settings → Screen Time for app limits and downtime
Android

Look for:

  • Settings → Apps & notifications → Permissions
  • Settings → Google Play Store → Parental controls
  • Settings → Digital Wellbeing for app timers
REVIEW PERIODICALLY

Privacy settings and platform features change. It can be useful to review them periodically, especially:

  • After a major app update
  • When adding a new device
  • If your account use or goals change
  • When age and independence shift (for young people)
Key Points
  • There's no single "correct" privacy setting that works for everyone. Your choices depend on who you are, what you use the platform for, and who you want to reach.
  • Understanding what a setting does is more important than following a checklist. Make choices that fit your comfort level and goals.
  • Privacy settings are just one part of online safety. Judgment and communication matter too.
