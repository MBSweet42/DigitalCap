🔐 Apps Kids Love: Privacy & Safety Settings

Grade Level: 3rd – 5th Grade
Duration: 45 minutes
Topic: Privacy & Data Protection
📋 Standards Alignment
ISTE Standards for Students Digital Citizen 2a, 2c: Cultivate digital identity and engage responsibly by managing privacy settings and personal information.
Common Core Standards SL.3.1 / SL.4.1 / SL.5.1: Engage effectively in collaborative discussions, building on ideas and clarifying thinking.
SEL Standards Responsible Decision-Making: Understand and manage personal privacy preferences and make informed choices about data sharing.
🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Identify privacy risks in apps (who can see their profile, who can contact them, data collection)
  • Locate privacy settings in common apps (Instagram, Snapchat, Discord, TikTok)
  • Explain why each privacy setting matters (protection, safety, boundaries)
  • Configure basic privacy settings independently with guidance
📦 Materials Needed
  • Laptops, tablets, or phones (one per pair or small group)
  • Projector to model app navigation
  • Handout: "Privacy Checklist for Popular Apps" (provided below)
  • Screenshots of app privacy settings (for reference)
  • Chart paper for creating a "Privacy Settings Guide"
⏱️ Lesson Timeline (45 minutes)
Opening: 5 minutes

Hook with a scenario about oversharing. Introduce the concept of privacy settings.

Teach: 10 minutes

Model how to access and explain 2-3 key privacy settings. Demonstration on projector.

Guided Practice: 15 minutes

Students work in pairs to explore settings on one app. Teacher circulates to support.

Discussion: 10 minutes

Class shares what they found. Why does each setting matter?

Wrap-up: 5 minutes

Action plan: Students will discuss with parents which settings to adjust at home.

📖 Full Lesson Plan
Opening: The Oversharing Scenario (5 minutes)

Present this scenario to students:

"Imagine your best friend posts a picture of you at their house on Instagram. They tag your location, so everyone can see where they live. A week later, someone they don't know sends them a message saying 'I know where you are.' How would that feel?"

Discuss: Why is this risky? What settings could have prevented this?

Key intro: "Every app has privacy settings—like locks on your doors. Today we're going to learn how to set those locks so only the people you want can see your stuff."

Teach: Where Are Privacy Settings? (10 minutes)

Show on projector and explain each setting:

App: Instagram / TikTok / Snapchat

  • Private Account: "When your account is private, only people you approve can see your posts. Random people can't follow you."
  • Who Can Message You: "You can limit DMs to only friends, not strangers."
  • Who Can See Your Story: "Your story can be seen by close friends only, not everyone."
  • Location Services: "You can turn this off so apps don't track where you are."
  • Comments & Tags: "You can decide who can comment on your posts and tag you in photos."

As you explain each, ask: "Why do you think this matters? How would this protect you?"

Important note: Settings change frequently. Use screenshots as backup references. Emphasize the "why" more than the exact steps.
Guided Practice: Explore One App (15 minutes)

👥 Partner Activity: Privacy Settings Hunt

Procedure:

  1. Divide class into pairs or small groups
  2. Assign each group one app (Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, Discord, etc.)
  3. Give them the "Privacy Checklist" handout
  4. Students explore the app's settings and check off which privacy options are available
  5. They discuss: What settings should be ON? Which OFF? Why?

Checklist items to find:

  • ☐ Private account option
  • ☐ Who can message me
  • ☐ Who can see my story
  • ☐ Location sharing (on/off)
  • ☐ Comments/tags control
  • ☐ Activity status (show when online or not)

Teacher role: Circulate. Answer questions. Praise when students find settings. Encourage discussion: "Why did you choose that setting?"

Discussion: Share & Reflect (10 minutes)

Bring class back together. Each group shares:

❓ Question 1: "What did you find? Which app had the most privacy options?"
❓ Question 2: "What setting did you think was most important? Why?"
❓ Question 3: "If someone has their account on public (not private), who can see their posts? Is that safe?"
❓ Question 4: "Why do apps let you control these settings? What happens if you don't?"

Key takeaways to reinforce:

  • Apps make money by collecting data and showing ads. Privacy settings help you protect your data.
  • Default settings are often set to "public" to maximize engagement. You have to change them.
  • Private accounts = you control who sees your stuff.
Wrap-Up & Action Plan (5 minutes)

Say to students:

"Apps are tools. They can be safe when you set them up right. This week, I want you to talk to your parents about one app you use. Look at the privacy settings together. Ask: 'Should this be private?' 'Who should be able to message me?' Make a plan to adjust settings if needed. You're learning to be in control of your own online safety!"

Send home the parent guide below.

💡 Implementation Notes
Differentiation
  • Struggling readers/tech users: Pair with stronger peers. Provide visual guides (screenshots with arrows).
  • Advanced learners: Challenge them to find a privacy setting not on the checklist. Research why apps default to "public."
  • No device access at home: Pair with peers who do. Or use class devices for practice.
Privacy Consideration for Teachers

Important: If you ask students to log into personal accounts:

  • Get parental permission first
  • Don't save any login information
  • Don't take screenshots of their profiles
  • Use demo accounts created specifically for teaching when possible
Extension Activities
  • Create a class "Privacy Settings Guide" on chart paper. Post it where families can see it (hallway, website).
  • Invite IT/tech staff to share how data is collected and used (age-appropriate explanation).
  • Monthly "Privacy Check-In": Remind students to review settings seasonally as apps update.
📧 Parent Send-Home Guide
Privacy Settings Setup: A Family Conversation

What We Learned in Class

This week, we taught students how to navigate privacy settings on popular apps (Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, Discord, etc.). The goal: empower them to control who sees their information online.

The Reality About App Settings

Apps default to "public" to maximize engagement and data collection. This means your child's posts, location, and activity are visible by default to everyone—unless they change the settings.

Essential Privacy Settings for Every App

  1. Private Account: Turn this ON. Only approved followers can see posts.
  2. Who Can Message Me: Set to "Friends Only" or "People I Follow."
  3. Story/Snap Visibility: Friends only, not public.
  4. Location Services: Turn OFF unless the app truly needs it.
  5. Activity Status: OFF (so people don't see when they're online).
  6. Comments & Tags: Approve tags before they appear. Limit who can comment.

How to Have This Conversation at Home

Step 1: "I'd like to look at the apps you use with you. Can you show me one?"

Step 2: Ask: "Is your account private or public? Who can see your posts?"

Step 3: Navigate to Settings together. Look for privacy options.

Step 4: Ask: "Who do you want to see this? Is this setting protecting that?" Make adjustments together.

Step 5: Praise them: "Great job learning to protect your privacy!"

Why This Matters

  • Privacy settings protect against strangers and unwanted contact
  • They limit data collection by companies
  • They teach kids to be intentional about what they share
  • They build digital responsibility skills

Red Flags

If your child:

  • Refuses to show you their apps or settings
  • Says "everyone leaves it public"
  • Is receiving messages from strangers
  • Seems pressured to share personal information

This is worth a deeper conversation. Don't shame. Ask: "Help me understand. Why do you feel you need to keep this public?"
