Q: At what age should I let my child have social media?

Asked by: Parent • Answered by: Dr. Sarah Chen, Digital Safety Expert

Expert Answer: Most child development experts recommend waiting until age 13-14 for social media, not because of apps' age limits, but because that's when kids develop the critical thinking skills to navigate social comparison, predators, and peer pressure. Before 13, the risks significantly outweigh the benefits. Even at 13+, heavy supervision and conversation are essential. The research is clear: kids who start social media before 13 show higher rates of anxiety and depression.

Q: How do I talk to my child about online predators without scaring them?

Asked by: Parent • Answered by: James Rodriguez, Child Safety Counselor

Expert Answer: Frame it as teaching, not scaring. Start with: "People online aren't always who they say they are. Adults sometimes pretend to be kids to make friends." Focus on solutions: "If someone asks personal questions, tells you they love you quickly, or asks to keep it secret—those are red flags. Tell me immediately." Make it clear that YOU won't be angry—you're their safety net. Kids who feel they can't talk to parents are at higher risk, so create that safe space.

Q: What should I do if I find explicit photos on my teen's phone?

Asked by: Parent • Answered by: Dr. Patricia Williams, Clinical Psychologist

Expert Answer: First: Stay calm and don't shame them. Second: Ask questions to understand the context—are they photos of themselves? Photos from partners? Unsolicited photos sent to them? The answers determine next steps. For self-taken explicit photos: educate on risks (sextortion, permanence, legal implications), have them delete, consider therapy. For photos sent by others: this may be exploitation—document and consider reporting. For revenge porn: document everything and contact police. The goal is safety, not punishment.
