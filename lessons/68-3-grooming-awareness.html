🚨 Stranger Danger & Grooming Awareness

Grade: 6-8
Duration: 45 min
🎯 Objectives
  • Recognize grooming tactics and red flags
  • Understand how predators manipulate teens
  • Know it's NOT their fault if approached by an adult
  • Know where to get help
📖 Key Content

What Is Grooming?

Definition: When an adult builds trust with a young person to manipulate them. Usually leads to sexual exploitation.

How it happens:

  • Phase 1 - Friendship: Adult compliments them, asks personal questions, seems to "understand" them
  • Phase 2 - Isolation: "This is our secret. Don't tell your parents."
  • Phase 3 - Manipulation: Requests for photos, sexual conversations, or in-person meeting
⚠️ CRITICAL: This is NEVER the young person's fault. The adult is always responsible.

Red Flags

If an online person:

  • ❌ Asks personal questions (name, school, location, age)
  • ❌ Says "your parents don't understand you, but I do"
  • ❌ Asks to keep the relationship secret
  • ❌ Sends gifts or money
  • ❌ Asks for photos or video chats
  • ❌ Wants to meet in person
  • ❌ Calls you "mature for your age"

What to do: Block them. Tell a trusted adult immediately. Save evidence (screenshots).

Real Talk

Reality: Online predators exist. They target middle and high schoolers. They're skilled at manipulation.

BUT: You can protect yourself by knowing the signs and telling a trusted adult immediately.

Resources:

  • CyberTipline.org - Report exploitation
  • NCMEC NetSmartz - Grooming awareness videos
  • School counselor - Always available
  • Police - If grooming is happening

Discussion Activity

Scenario: "Someone online says they're 14, from your area, and thinks you're cool. They ask for your Snapchat. What's your gut feeling? Why?"

Discuss: Trust your instincts. If something feels off, it probably is.

Parent Guide: Grooming Awareness

The Reality

Online predators specifically target teens. They groom them with attention, understanding, and flattery before exploitation.

Warning Signs Your Teen Is Being Groomed

  • Secretive about online activity
  • New "friend" they won't introduce you to
  • Receiving gifts or money
  • Emotional changes (mood swings, secrecy, anxiety)
  • Inappropriate conversations about age, looks, relationships

If You Suspect Grooming

  1. Stay calm. Don't shame your teen.
  2. Get evidence (screenshots)
  3. Block the person
  4. Report to the platform AND police
  5. Contact: CyberTipline.org or 1-800-THE-LOST

Prevention at Home

Know what apps your teen uses. Be their friend on social media (they might not like it, but do it anyway). Have open conversations about online friends. Make it safe for them to tell you if something weird happens.
