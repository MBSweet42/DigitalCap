⏰ Screen Time & Sleep Guide
Why screen time matters, healthy habits, and how to enforce limits without conflict
📊 RECOMMENDED SCREEN TIME BY AGE
Ages 6-8
1-2 hours per day of quality content
Focus on educational programs and co-viewing when possible
Why:
Young brains are still developing. Limited screen time allows more time for physical play, social interaction, and creative play—all essential for healthy development.
Ages 9-12
1-2.5 hours per day, balanced with other activities
Mix entertainment with educational content. Monitor content closely.
Why:
This age group is more susceptible to algorithm-driven addiction and social comparison. Strong limits now prevent unhealthy habits later.
Ages 13-15
2-3 hours per day with established boundaries
Teens need more independence, but structure still matters. Avoid screens 1 hour before bed.
Why:
Teen brains are developing judgment and impulse control. Boundaries teach healthy self-regulation before they leave home.
🛏️ THE SLEEP CONNECTION

Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin (the sleep hormone). This is why screen use before bed ruins sleep quality.

  • No screens 1 hour before bed — Switch to reading, conversation, or quiet activities
  • No phones in the bedroom — Keep devices in a common charging station overnight
  • Don't use screens to fall asleep — This creates dependency and disrupts sleep cycles
  • Recommend 8-10 hours of sleep for kids — This requires bedtimes early enough to achieve this
✅ STRATEGIES FOR HEALTHY LIMITS
  • Set device-free times: Meals, car rides, and family time are phone-free zones for EVERYONE
  • Use parental controls: App limits, screen time tracking, and content filters are your tools
  • Model good behavior: If you're constantly on your phone, your child will be too
  • Make the case together: Explain WHY limits exist (not just "because I said so")
  • Offer alternatives: Boredom drives screen use. Have real activities ready: board games, outdoor time, hobbies
  • Avoid using screens as a reward/punishment: This makes them seem more valuable
⚠️ RED FLAGS FOR ADDICTION

If your child shows any of these, screen time may be unhealthy:

  • Extreme moodiness when screens are taken away
  • Lies about how much time they've been using screens
  • Neglecting homework, friends, sports, or sleep for screen time
  • Can't sit through a meal without their phone
  • Declining grades or loss of interest in previous hobbies
💡 If you see these signs:
Have a calm conversation about what's happening. Consider a temporary (1-2 week) screen break. If the issue persists, talk to your pediatrician—addiction is real and treatable.
