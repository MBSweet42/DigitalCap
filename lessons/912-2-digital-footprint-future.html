👣 Digital Footprint & Future Implications

Grade: 9-12
Duration: 45 min
🎯 Objectives
  • Understand that digital footprint affects real-world opportunities (college, jobs, relationships)
  • Audit their own online presence
  • Make intentional choices about what to share NOW
  • Think 5, 10, 20 years ahead
📖 Lesson Content

The Reality

What happens to posts you make now:

  • Screenshots last forever
  • Colleges check social media during admissions
  • Employers Google applicants before hiring
  • Background checks include online searches
  • The internet has a long memory

Case Studies

Case 1: A teen's old tweets with racial slurs resurface 6 years later during college applications. College revokes admission.

Case 2: Employer finds explicit photos from a job candidate's old Instagram. They don't get hired.

Case 3: A hurtful post from age 14 is shared by 100,000 people when the person is applying for a scholarship at 18.

Action: Audit Your Footprint

What to do:

  1. Google yourself (your full name)
  2. Search on social platforms (Instagram, TikTok, Twitter, Facebook, YouTube)
  3. Look for old accounts you forgot about
  4. Document what you find
  5. Delete or hide things you regret
  6. Adjust privacy settings

Moving forward: Before posting, ask: "Would I want a college admissions officer or future employer seeing this?"

The Long Game

Think about these timelines:

  • 1 year: Your friends remember what you posted
  • 2-5 years: College admissions see it
  • 5-10 years: Employers see it
  • 20+ years: It's still discoverable somewhere

Real talk: You're not ruined if you've posted something embarrassing. But be thoughtful NOW about what you're creating.

Family Conversation: Digital Permanence

Questions for Your Teen

  • "What would future you (at age 25, 35, 50) want you to know about your digital footprint right now?"
  • "If colleges could see everything you post, would you change anything?"
  • "What posts from your friends do you think they might regret later?"

Parent Action

Show them your own digital footprint. Be honest: "I'm not proud of some things I posted online. That's why I'm careful now." Model what good digital citizenship looks like at any age.
