📖 Full Lesson

Opening: What's Cyberbullying?

Define: Cyberbullying is mean, hurtful, or threatening messages sent online. It can be:

  • Mean comments on posts or photos
  • Spreading rumors online
  • Excluding someone from group chats
  • Sharing embarrassing photos or videos
  • Impersonating someone online

Key difference from in-person bullying: Cyberbullying is PUBLIC (many people see it), PERMANENT (screenshots last forever), and RELENTLESS (messages keep coming).

Teach: The Bystander Effect

Three ways people respond to cyberbullying:

  • 🔴 Upstander (Best): Shows kindness, tells the person it's not OK, reports it, tells a grown-up
  • 🟡 Bystander (Not helping): Sees it but doesn't do anything. Likes or retweets mean comments.
  • 🔴 Perpetrator (Worst): Posts mean comments, spreads rumors, laughs along

The power of being an upstander: When even one person stands up and shows kindness, the target feels less alone. Cyberbullies often stop when they see others won't participate.

Activity: Practice Kind Responses

💬 What Would You Say?

Read each scenario. Ask students: "What's a kind thing you could say or do?"

  1. Scenario: Your classmate posts a photo and gets mean comments calling them ugly.
    Kind response: "Your photo is great! I think you're awesome."
  2. Scenario: Someone spreads a false rumor about a classmate online.
    Kind response: Comment: "That's not true. Let's be kind."
  3. Scenario: A kid is left out of a group chat on purpose.
    Kind response: Include them. Send them a message: "Want to join?"

Role-play these responses together!

Wrap-Up: Action Plan

If you witness cyberbullying:

✔ DO:

  • Show kindness to the target
  • Tell a trusted adult
  • Report it to the platform
  • Don't like, share, or retweet mean comments
📧 Parent Send-Home Guide
Cyberbullying: How to Help Your Child

What We Learned

Cyberbullying is online harassment. Unlike in-person bullying, it's public, permanent, and relentless. We taught students to be "upstanders"—people who stand up against bullying with kindness.

Warning Signs Your Child Is Being Bullied

  • Withdrawn from friends or activities
  • Anxious about using their phone or computer
  • Shares hurtful comments they received
  • Changes in sleep, eating, or mood

How to Help

1. Listen without judgment. "I'm so sorry this is happening. This is not your fault."

2. Don't take away the phone! You need access to evidence. Instead, help them document it (screenshots).

3. Report it. To the platform and to the school.

4. Involve authorities if needed. Threats or explicit content require law enforcement.

If Your Child Is the One Being Mean Online

Stay calm. Have a conversation about empathy. Help them understand the impact of their words. Natural consequences (losing device privileges) are appropriate. Consider: Why did they feel the need to be mean?
