👣 Your Digital Footprint Starts Now

Grade Level: Kindergarten – 2nd Grade
Duration: 15 minutes
Topic: Privacy & Digital Footprint
📋 Standards Alignment
ISTE Standards for Students Digital Citizen 2c: Demonstrate good digital citizenship by treating others with respect and keeping personal information private.
Common Core Standards W.K.2 / W.1.2 / W.2.2: Use a combination of drawing, dictating, and writing to compose informative texts.
SEL Standards Responsible Decision-Making: Make choices about personal information and understand consequences.
🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

  • Understand that what they do online creates a permanent record
  • Identify examples of digital footprints (pictures, posts, videos)
  • Recognize that what's online can be seen by many people
  • Practice making good choices online by thinking before sharing
📦 Materials Needed
  • Chart paper or whiteboard
  • Markers in various colors
  • Pictures of footprints (sand footprints or snow footprints are perfect metaphors)
  • Printable activity sheets
  • Optional: Play-Doh or sand in a sensory bin to make literal footprints
⏱️ Lesson Timeline (15 minutes)
Opening: 3 minutes

Introduce the concept of "digital footprints" using a relatable metaphor.

Teach: 5 minutes

Explain what a digital footprint is with concrete examples.

Discuss: 3 minutes

Ask students about their digital footprints and why they matter.

Activity: 3 minutes

Students practice making good digital choices.

Wrap-up: 1 minute

Key takeaway about thinking before sharing.

📖 Full Lesson Plan
Opening: Real Footprints (3 minutes)

Start with a concrete metaphor:

"Imagine you're playing outside in the sand or snow. You walk around, and your footprints show where you've been, right? Well, online things work the same way! When you use screens—like when you play games, watch videos, or send messages—you leave digital footprints. These are like tracks that show what you did online."

Show pictures of footprints in sand or snow. Talk about how the footprints stay there for a while.

Key idea: "Just like footprints in the sand, things you do online stay there too. People can see them."

Teach: What's a Digital Footprint? (5 minutes)

Explain with examples students can understand:

  • Pictures: "If your parents post a photo of you online, that's part of your digital footprint."
  • Videos: "If you're in a video someone shares, that's part of your digital footprint."
  • Comments: "If you write something online, people can see it. That's part of your footprint."
  • Games: "The games you play and the things you do in them—people can sometimes see that too."

The big idea:

"Your digital footprint is the trail of things you've done online. And here's the important part: it stays there. It's like writing in permanent marker instead of pencil. You can erase pencil, but permanent marker stays. So we have to think before we do things online."

Age-appropriate language: Avoid scary words like "forever" or "permanent." Instead say: "It stays there for a long time, so it's important to make good choices."
Discuss: Your Digital Footprint (3 minutes)
❓ Question 1: "What kinds of things leave digital footprints? (pictures, videos, games, messages)"
❓ Question 2: "If you post a picture online, who might see it? (Your parents, friends, their parents, maybe people you don't know)"
❓ Question 3: "Why is it important to think before we post or share something online?"

Listen to their answers. Affirm ideas like:

  • "It might stay there a long time"
  • "Lots of people could see it"
  • "We want people to see us being kind and smart"
Activity: "Good Footprint, Not-So-Good Footprint" (3 minutes)

🎯 Scenario Sorting

Read each scenario. Students say "Good footprint!" or "Not a good footprint!"

  1. Scenario 1: "You're playing a game and you help your friend win." → GOOD FOOTPRINT!
  2. Scenario 2: "You post a mean comment about someone." → NOT A GOOD FOOTPRINT
  3. Scenario 3: "Your mom takes a picture of you doing something you're proud of." → GOOD FOOTPRINT!
  4. Scenario 4: "You share your friend's picture without asking them first." → NOT A GOOD FOOTPRINT
  5. Scenario 5: "You send a kind message to a friend." → GOOD FOOTPRINT!

After each scenario, ask: "How would this footprint make you feel? What about other people? What could we do instead?"

Wrap-Up & Key Takeaway (1 minute)

Say to students:

"Your digital footprints are the trail of things you do online. They stay there, and people can see them. So let's make good footprints! Before you post something, share a picture, or send a message, ask yourself: 'Would I be proud of this footprint? Would I want my family to see it?' If the answer is yes, you're making a good digital footprint. Great job!"

💡 Implementation Notes
Differentiation
  • Younger Students (K-Early 1st): Use only the footprint metaphor. Skip complex scenarios. Focus on: "Good choices online = good footprints."
  • Advanced Learners: Discuss how digital footprints affect others. Example: "If you share someone's picture, you're creating a footprint for them too. It's their footprint too!"
  • Visual Learners: Draw footprints on chart paper. Have students add words or pictures to show good digital choices.
Hands-On Extension

Optional sensory activity: If time allows, have students make footprints in sand or Play-Doh to literally see how footprints stay. Talk about how online footprints are the same way—they stick around!

Parent Involvement

Encourage parents to:

  • Ask their child before posting pictures online
  • Show their child examples of good digital footprints (kind comments, helpful posts)
  • Explain why they're careful about what they share about their child
📧 Parent Send-Home Guide
Your Child's Digital Footprint

What We Learned in Class

This week, we talked about "digital footprints"—the trail of things we do online. Just like footprints in sand show where you've walked, digital footprints show what you've done online. And they stay there!

The Big Idea

Everything your child does online creates a digital footprint: videos, games, pictures, messages, comments. These footprints can be seen by many people and can last a long time. Teaching kids to make good digital footprints is an important early lesson in online responsibility.

What Counts as a Digital Footprint?

  • Pictures and videos (posted by them or by you about them)
  • Comments and messages
  • Games they play and achievements
  • Videos they watch (yes, YouTube recommends based on this!)
  • Information they share (names, ages, school, location)

How to Help at Home

1. Ask before posting. Before you share photos or information about your child online, ask yourself: "Am I comfortable with this being public? Could this affect my child later?"

2. Monitor what they see. If they use screens, know what apps and games they're using. Ask: "What did you see today? Who were you talking to?"

3. Teach them to think before sharing. Ask: "Is this something you'd be proud of? Could it hurt anyone's feelings?"

4. Model good digital footprints. Be kind online. Don't post mean comments. Show your child that you care about your own digital footprint.

Red Flags

Talk to your child's teacher or school counselor if:

  • Your child is sharing personal information online
  • They're posting unkind things about others
  • They seem worried or upset about something online
