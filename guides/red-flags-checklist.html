🚩 Red Flags Checklist
Warning signs that something might be wrong online. Keep this handy and trust your gut.
BEHAVIORAL RED FLAGS
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Secretive behavior with devices
Your child quickly closes apps or hides their screen when you walk by. Uses passwords you don't know about. Gets defensive when asked about what they're doing online.
What to do: Have a calm conversation. It could be innocent (they like privacy) or serious (they're hiding something). Ask gently: "I noticed you close your screen. Is everything okay?"
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Extreme mood swings related to device use
Your child is happy while online but angry, anxious, or sad when they have to stop. They cry or throw tantrums when devices are taken away. They seem obsessed with checking their phone.
What to do: This suggests possible addiction or cyberbullying. Reduce screen time gradually. Talk to your pediatrician if the behavior continues.
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Sleep problems, especially refusing to put down their phone
Your child stays up past midnight on their device. They're exhausted but refuse to stop. They sneak their phone into bed. Their grades are declining due to sleep deprivation.
What to do: Enforce a no-screens-in-bedroom rule. Use parental controls to disable internet after bedtime. This is often a sign of addiction.
⚠️ MEDIUM CONCERN
Neglecting real-world activities
Your child has stopped playing sports, seeing friends in person, or doing hobbies they used to love. They only want to be online. Their grades are dropping.
What to do: Have a conversation about balance. Consider reducing screen time and encouraging offline activities.
SOCIAL RED FLAGS
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
New online "friends" you've never heard of
Your child suddenly has new friends from online. They don't know them in real life. They spend a lot of time chatting with them privately. You've never video called to verify they're real kids.
What to do: Ask to meet these friends via video call. Ask your child questions about them. Adults posing as kids is common—trust your instincts.
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Receiving gifts or money from online friends
An online "friend" is sending your child gift cards, money, or buying them in-game items. They're being unusually nice or generous.
What to do: This is a classic grooming tactic. DO NOT accept the gifts. Block the person immediately. Report them to the platform. Tell law enforcement if this continues.
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Being asked to keep secrets from parents
An online person is asking your child not to tell you about them. Phrases like "your parents wouldn't understand" or "this is just between us" are RED FLAGS.
What to do: This is grooming behavior. Block immediately. Report to law enforcement if the person is sexual in nature.
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Being cyberbullied or sent mean messages
Your child receives hurtful comments, threats, or rumors about them spread online. They're being excluded or ganged up on. They mention wanting to hurt themselves.
What to do: Screenshot everything. Report to the platform. Contact school if bullies are classmates. If self-harm is mentioned, call 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) immediately.
⚠️ MEDIUM CONCERN
Constant comparison and low self-esteem
Your child compares themselves to others online. They're anxious about likes and comments. They say negative things about themselves: "I'm ugly," "Nobody likes me."
What to do: Consider limiting time on comparison-heavy apps (Instagram, TikTok). Talk about how social media is a highlight reel, not reality.
CONTENT RED FLAGS
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Accessing or sharing sexual content
You find images, videos, or text of a sexual nature. Your child is sending or receiving nudes. They're being pressured to share intimate photos.
What to do: Do not delete evidence. Contact local law enforcement immediately. This is a federal crime if minors are involved.
⚠️ HIGH CONCERN
Watching concerning or disturbing content
You notice your child watching violent, graphic, or disturbing videos. They're seeking content related to self-harm or eating disorders.
What to do: Use parental controls to block the content. Have a conversation about why it's harmful. If they're expressing self-harm thoughts, contact a mental health professional.
🚨 IF YOU SEE THESE, ACT IMMEDIATELY:
✓ Sexual contact with minors or attempted contact
✓ Threats of violence or harm
✓ Child is being blackmailed or extorted
✓ Child is expressing suicidal thoughts
CONTACT LAW ENFORCEMENT: 911 or your local police department
NATIONAL CENTER FOR MISSING & EXPLOITED CHILDREN: CyberTipline.org
SUICIDE CRISIS: Call 988
