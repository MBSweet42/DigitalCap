Heard Something About AI?

AI claims spread quickly. Some are true, some are false, and a lot of them need context.

You don't have to become an AI expert. Slow down, check the claim, and look at what is actually happening.

You may have heard:

"You can always spot an AI image by looking at the hands."

What's actually happening

AI-generated images sometimes contain visual mistakes, but those clues are not reliable proof. AI systems improve, real photos can look strange, and edited images can create the same kinds of odd details.

Also important

A realistic image is not proof that something happened. A strange-looking image is not proof that AI made it.

What you can do

Check the source, search for the original, look for independent evidence, and use visual clues as clues rather than proof.

Learn how to verify what you see →

You may have heard:

"ChatGPT trains on everything you say."

What's actually happening

Not universally. How information may be used depends on your account, settings, the feature you are using, and how the information entered ChatGPT.

Also important

Model training, chat history, Memory, connected apps, storage, and sharing are different things.

What you can do

Check your Data Controls and Memory settings, understand what connected apps can access, and think before sharing sensitive information.

Understand ChatGPT privacy controls →

You may have heard:

"Every AI prompt uses a bottle of water."

What's actually happening

AI and data centers can use water directly for cooling and indirectly through electricity generation, but there is no single universal amount of water used by every prompt.

Also important

Water use can vary enormously depending on the workload, hardware, cooling system, location, electricity source, utilization, and other infrastructure factors.

What you can do

Be cautious with precise per-prompt claims that do not explain how the number was calculated or what system it describes.

How DigitalCap Approaches AI

AI is not automatically dangerous. AI is not automatically trustworthy. AI can be useful. AI can make mistakes. Understanding how it works gives people more control over how they use it.

Our role is to explain what the technology does, what it can be useful for, what limitations or risks exist, what choices people have, what actions they can take, and where information came from.

This section is for anyone—adults, teens, families, caregivers, older adults, educators, and anyone trying to navigate AI in daily life.
