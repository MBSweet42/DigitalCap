Build Your Family's Digital Agreement

An interactive worksheet to help families discuss devices, expectations, and what you agree on together.

There isn't one digital agreement that works for every household. This tool helps families choose the topics that matter to them, talk through their expectations, and write down what they decide. The agreement can change as needs, circumstances, independence, and age change.

You don't need to fill this out all at once. Pick topics that matter most to your household, have the conversation, and write down what you agree on.

Select the topics you want to include in your agreement. You can select as many as you want and add more later.

In-game chat? Multiplayer with strangers? In-game purchases? Time limits? Game age ratings?

Photos & sharing

Sharing friends' photos without asking? Posting videos? Types of content that should never be shared?

When problems happen

If something uncomfortable, confusing, unsafe, or unexpected happens online, asking for help should be easier than hiding the problem. This section is about how to handle those moments with safety and support first.

Who can they talk to if something goes wrong?

Is there more than one trusted adult? How should they start the conversation?

What happens first when there's a problem?

Handle immediate safety first. Block, remove contact, leave. Preserve evidence if needed. Tell who needs to know.

DigitalCap Respond has step-by-step guidance for mean comments, uncomfortable contact, pressure, and other situations. Consider reviewing it together.

Broken agreements

When an agreement isn't followed, there's usually a reason to address it. But the response depends on what happened and what makes sense for your household. Different families handle this differently, and that's okay.

If an agreement is broken, what response makes sense?

A conversation? Temporary changes? Repairing harm? Checking if the rule is realistic?

Growing independence

As people grow, their privacy, independence, responsibility, and supervision can change. These changes should happen thoughtfully and with communication.

What responsibilities or behaviors show readiness for more independence?

Honesty about device use? Good decisions without supervision? Following agreements? Asking for help?

Which protections or supervision can change over time?

Password sharing? Location sharing? App approval? Screen time limits?

Which safety practices should stay the same?

What will you always discuss or check in about, regardless of age?

Tips for making this work

  • Create expectations together when possible. People are more likely to follow agreements they helped create.
  • Start with what matters most. You don't need to fill out every section at once.
  • Adults can model the habits you're discussing. If device-free time matters, adults participate too.
  • Revisit agreements regularly. Plans may change as people grow, as new devices arrive, or as circumstances shift.
  • Clarity is often more useful than assumptions. A conversation about what you each expect is worth more than guessing.
  • Rules should help support safety and healthy use, not make someone afraid to ask for help. The point is connection and understanding, not control.
