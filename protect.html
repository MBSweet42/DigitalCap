Setting Up a New Device

You don't need to figure everything out at once. Start with these areas in order, using the DigitalCap guidance that's already available.

1. Start with what's appropriate for their age

Age changes what protections, limits, apps, and conversations make sense. Start with DigitalCap's age-based guidance so you have a baseline for the rest of the setup.

See guidance for my child's age →

2. Set the important protections

Review privacy, who can contact your child, location sharing, app permissions, and the device-level controls available to you.

Review privacy settings → Set up device controls →

3. Agree on expectations before problems come up

Set expectations around downloads, purchases, bedtime, privacy, and what your child should do if something online makes them uncomfortable.

Review Family Device Rules →

4. Talk about why the protections are there

Rules and settings work better when your child understands why they exist. Use DigitalCap's conversation help to make the discussion feel like an ongoing part of device use, not just a list of restrictions.

Start a Conversation → or browse printable starters

Make Their Current Device Safer

If your child already uses a phone, tablet, or computer, you don't have to start over. Review the areas below and focus first on anything that doesn't match your family's needs.

1. Review privacy and who can contact them

Start by checking who can see your child's accounts, who can message them, whether location is shared, and which permissions their apps currently have.

Review privacy settings → or check a specific app

2. Check the device controls already in place

Review the device-level controls available for screen time, content, purchases, and app access, then decide whether the current setup still fits your child.

Review device controls →

3. Revisit your family's rules and boundaries

A device that was set up months or years ago may no longer match your family's expectations. Review rules around downloads, purchases, bedtime, privacy, and what happens when something feels unsafe.

Review Family Device Rules →

4. Notice changes that may be worth checking in on

Changes in behavior, sleep, privacy, online friendships, or device habits can sometimes be a reason to check in. Context matters, and one change by itself does not necessarily mean something is wrong.

Review Warning Signs →
