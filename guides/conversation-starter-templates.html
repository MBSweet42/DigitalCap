💬 Conversation Starter Templates
40+ conversation prompts to start tough talks about digital safety
Ages 6-8
About Apps & Games
"What's your favorite app? Can you show me what you like about it?"
Follow-up: "Do you ever chat with people you don't know in that game?"
About Screen Time
"How much time do you think you spend on your device each day?"
Follow-up: "How do you feel when it's time to stop using it?"
About Online Kindness
"If someone was mean to you online, would you tell me?"
Follow-up: "What would you want me to do to help?"
About Passwords
"Do you know why we use passwords on our accounts?"
Follow-up: "Should we ever share our passwords? When is it okay?"
Ages 9-12
About Social Media
"Are you on any social media apps? Tell me about them."
Follow-up: "Who do you follow? Do you know them in real life?"
About Online Privacy
"What information do you share online—your real name, location, school?"
Follow-up: "Why might we want to keep some things private?"
About Cyberbullying
"Have you or has anyone you know experienced mean comments online?"
Follow-up: "What's a good way to handle it if it happens to you?"
About In-App Spending
"Do any of your games have things you can buy inside the app?"
Follow-up: "Have you ever wanted to buy something? Why?"
Ages 13-15
About Online Identity
"How do you present yourself differently online vs. in person?"
Follow-up: "Do you think people always tell the truth online?"
About Inappropriate Contact
"Has anyone online ever asked you personal questions that felt weird?"
Follow-up: "You can always tell me. I'm here to help, not punish."
About Digital Footprint
"What would a stranger learn about you by looking at your posts?"
Follow-up: "Would you be comfortable with colleges or employers seeing that?"
About FOMO & Comparison
"Do you ever feel like you're missing out when you're not online?"
Follow-up: "How does comparing yourself to others online make you feel?"
💡 HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE
Pick one conversation starter per week. Choose a calm moment (car ride, dinner, before bed). Listen more than you talk. Ask follow-up questions. Thank them for being honest. Most importantly: keep the conversation going regularly, not just when there's a problem.
