🛡️ Recognizing Concerning Online Behavior
K-2
15 min
Safety & Trust
📋 Lesson Overview

This lesson teaches young children to recognize behaviors that feel uncomfortable or concerning in online interactions. Rather than promoting fear of "strangers," this research-based approach helps children identify specific warning signs and encourages them to trust their instincts and communicate with trusted adults.

🎯 Learning Objectives
  • Identify specific concerning behaviors in online interactions (asking for personal info, wanting secrecy, being overly friendly)
  • Understand that concerning behavior can come from anyone, not just "strangers"
  • Recognize and trust their "gut feeling" when something feels wrong
  • Know that telling a trusted adult is always the right choice
  • Understand that it's never the child's fault if an adult behaves in a concerning way
📦 Materials Needed
  • Whiteboard/chart paper
  • Markers
  • Picture cards or simple drawings showing different online scenarios
  • Optional: tablet/computer for showing examples (with appropriate content)
⏱️ Lesson Timeline (15 minutes)
Opening (2 min)

"Today we're going to talk about something important: how to stay safe when we're online. We're going to learn about behaviors that might feel weird or uncomfortable, and what to do when we notice them."

Teaching: Red Flag Behaviors (5 min)

Explain each behavior in simple language:

  • Asking for private information: "Someone online asks 'What's your name? Where do you live? What school do you go to?' These questions can make us feel uncomfortable because good online friends don't need that information."
  • Wanting to keep secrets: "If someone online says 'Don't tell your mom and dad about our chats,' that's a red flag. Real friends don't ask us to keep secrets from the grown-ups we trust."
  • Being too friendly too fast: "If someone you just met online is being extra special nice and giving you lots of compliments, that can feel nice but it's a warning sign. Real friendships take time."
  • Asking for pictures or personal things: "Never share pictures or personal information with someone online, even if they say they're your age."
Teaching: Trust Your Gut (3 min)

"You know your body. If something feels weird, uncomfortable, or scary - that's important. Your feelings are telling you something. You can always trust that feeling and tell a grown-up you trust."

Key message: "It's NEVER your fault if an adult online behaves in a concerning way. It's ALWAYS okay to tell a grown-up."

Discussion & Role-Play (3 min)

Present simple scenarios:

  • "A person online you don't know asks for your last name. What do you do?"
  • "Someone online says they'll be your special friend if you don't tell your parents. What do you do?"
  • "An online friend keeps asking for pictures. What do you do?"

Correct answer for all: "Don't give the information, tell a trusted grown-up right away."

Closing (2 min)

"You are smart and you can take care of yourself. If anything feels wrong online, tell a grown-up you trust. You're never in trouble for telling - ever."

💡 Teaching Tips
  • Keep language age-appropriate and avoid scary stories
  • Emphasize that the child is never at fault
  • Focus on specific behaviors, not fear of people
  • Empower children to trust their instincts
  • Reinforce that telling an adult is always the right choice
  • Connect to real-world safety ("Your body tells you when something feels wrong")

  • SEL Competencies: Self-awareness, responsible decision-making, relationship skills
  • Common Sense Media: Digital Citizenship framework
📧 Parent Send-Home Guide

🛡️ Recognizing Concerning Online Behavior

What Your Child Learned Today:

In today's lesson, your child learned to recognize specific behaviors that might feel uncomfortable or concerning when interacting online. Rather than teaching fear, we focused on helping children identify red flags and trust their instincts.

Red Flag Behaviors to Discuss:

  • Someone asking for personal information (name, address, school)
  • Someone wanting conversations to be kept secret from parents
  • Someone being overly friendly or giving excessive compliments
  • Someone asking for pictures or personal items

How to Support at Home:

  • Talk casually about their online activities: "Who do you play with online?"
  • Reinforce that they can always tell you if something feels wrong
  • Teach them that it's never their fault if someone acts inappropriately
  • Model healthy online behavior
  • Keep devices in common areas when possible

If Your Child Reports Concerning Behavior:

  • Stay calm and don't blame the child
  • Listen without judgment
  • Take action: block the person, report to the platform
  • Contact local authorities if needed (CyberTipline.org)

Key Message: "You can always tell me if something feels wrong. It's never your fault."
